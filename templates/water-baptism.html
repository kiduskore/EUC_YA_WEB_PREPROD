Water Baptism

What Is Baptism?

Let's clear something up right away: baptism doesn't save you — Jesus does. Baptism is not a requirement for getting into heaven, but it is an act of obedience to the One who saved you. Before Jesus ascended, He gave this command: "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19).

Peter echoed this on the day the Church was born: "Repent and be baptized, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins" (Acts 2:38). Baptism is your public declaration — an outward picture of an inward reality.

The symbolism is powerful and intentional. When you go under the water, it represents the burial of your old life — the person you were before Christ, with all of its guilt, shame, and brokenness, buried with Him. When you come up out of the water, it represents resurrection — you are raised to walk in the newness of life that Jesus purchased for you.

Paul captures it perfectly: "We were therefore buried with Him through baptism into death in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, we too may live a new life" (Romans 6:3-4). Baptism is a physical, tangible act that tells the world: the old me is gone, and I am alive in Christ.

Why Get Baptized?

First and most importantly: because Jesus did it. When Jesus came to John at the Jordan River, He didn't need to be baptized — He was sinless. But He chose to step into those waters to "fulfill all righteousness" and model what obedience looks like (Matthew 3:13-17).

And when He came up out of the water, the heavens opened, the Spirit of God descended like a dove, and the Father spoke audibly: "This is my Son, whom I love; with Him I am well pleased." If Jesus considered baptism important enough to do Himself, it matters.

Baptism is your first act of obedience as a follower of Jesus. It's a bold, counter-cultural, public statement that you are no longer living for yourself — you belong to Christ. In a world where everyone curates their identity online, baptism is the most honest post you'll ever make: "I was lost, and now I'm found." Look at the early church — when the Ethiopian eunuch heard the gospel from Philip, his immediate response was, "Look, here is water.

What can stand in the way of my being baptized?" (Acts 8:36-38). He didn't wait for a special program or perfect timing. He heard the truth, believed it, and acted on it.

That same urgency should mark us today.

Who Should Be Baptized?

If you have made a personal decision to follow Jesus Christ as your Lord and Savior, you are ready to be baptized. Full stop. It doesn't matter how old you are, what your background is, how long you've been a Christian, or how messy your story has been.

Baptism isn't for people who have it all together — it's for people who have surrendered to the One who does. You don't need to complete a theological degree or memorize a certain number of verses first. You just need faith in Jesus.

Maybe you gave your life to Christ years ago and never got baptized. Maybe you were baptized as an infant but have since made a personal, conscious decision to follow Jesus and want to make that declaration yourself. Maybe you literally just prayed the salvation prayer for the first time this week.

Wherever you are in that journey, if you believe that Jesus is Lord and you've turned your life over to Him, the water is for you. Don't let overthinking or insecurity keep you from obedience. The enemy would love nothing more than for you to delay this step indefinitely.

What to Expect

Baptism Sunday is one of the most joy-filled, Spirit-charged moments you'll ever experience. Here's what it looks like practically: wear comfortable clothes you don't mind getting soaked in — dark colors are best, and we'll provide a towel and a change area. You'll be guided into the water by a pastor or ministry leader who will ask you to share briefly why you've decided to follow Jesus.

Then they'll lower you under the water and bring you back up. It takes seconds, but it echoes into eternity.

When you come up out of that water, expect the room to erupt. Your church family — many of whom have walked the same road you're walking now — will be cheering, clapping, and praising God over your decision. It's not a solemn, quiet ritual.

It's a celebration. It's a family welcoming a brother or sister into the visible body of Christ. Some people cry.

Some people shout. Some people just grin from ear to ear because for the first time, the weight of their old identity has been publicly laid down. You'll remember this day for the rest of your life.

Don't miss it.
