Membership

Why Membership Matters

The New Testament doesn't know anything about isolated, free-agent Christians. When Paul wrote his letters, he wrote them to churches — specific, named, local communities of believers who were committed to one another. The church is described as a body where every part matters and no one can say "I don't need you" (1 Corinthians 12:12-27).

It's called a family where we bear one another's burdens and do good especially to those in the household of faith (Galatians 6:2, 10). Peter calls us living stones being built together into a spiritual house (1 Peter 2:5). Membership isn't a religious formality or a country club card — it's you saying, "I'm choosing to be known here.

I'm choosing to be accountable here. I'm planting my life in this soil and letting my roots go deep." In a culture that keeps its options open and avoids commitment, choosing to belong to a local church is one of the most counter-cultural and spiritually powerful decisions you can make.

What We Believe

We believe the Bible is the inspired, authoritative, and sufficient Word of God — the final standard for everything we believe and how we live (2 Timothy 3:16-17). We believe in one God who exists eternally in three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — each fully God, each distinct, working together in perfect unity for our redemption. We believe that salvation is by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone — not by our works, our goodness, or our effort, but entirely by the finished work of Jesus on the cross (Ephesians 2:8-9).

We believe the Holy Spirit is alive and active today, convicting us of sin, empowering us for holiness, distributing gifts for the building up of the church, and sealing us for the day of redemption (Ephesians 1:13-14). And we believe that Jesus Christ is coming back — bodily, visibly, and gloriously — to make all things new, to judge the living and the dead, and to establish His Kingdom forever (Revelation 21:5). These aren't abstract theological ideas.

They are the bedrock truths that shape how we worship, how we suffer, how we love, and how we hope.

The Membership Covenant

Membership is a two-way covenant — it's what you commit to the church and what the church commits to you. As a member, you're committing to gather consistently with the body for worship and teaching, because the writer of Hebrews warns us not to neglect meeting together (Hebrews 10:25). You're committing to serve with your gifts, to give generously and sacrificially, to pursue personal holiness by the power of the Spirit, to live in authentic community, and to submit to the pastoral leadership God has placed over this house (Hebrews 13:17).

That word "submit" might feel uncomfortable — but it simply means being teachable, being open to correction, and trusting that God works through imperfect leaders who love you and will give an account for your soul. In return, the church commits to shepherd you, pray for you, care for you in crisis, equip you for the work of ministry (Ephesians 4:11-12), and walk with you through every season — the mountaintops and the valleys. This covenant isn't legalism.

It's love with structure. It's family with intentionality.

Your Next Step

If something in your spirit is stirring, don't ignore it — that might be the Holy Spirit drawing you to plant your life here. Your next step is simple: attend our next membership class, where you'll learn more about our vision, our values, and what it practically looks like to belong. You'll meet the pastors, hear their hearts, ask your hardest questions, and have space to discern whether this is the community God is calling you to.

The Psalmist wrote, "Those who are planted in the house of the Lord shall flourish in the courts of our God" (Psalm 92:13). You weren't made to drift from church to church, livestream to livestream. You were made to be planted — and planted things grow.
