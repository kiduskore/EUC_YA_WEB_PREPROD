Generosity

God's Heart for Generosity

Second Corinthians 9:7 tells us that "God loves a cheerful giver." Notice it doesn't say God tolerates reluctant givers or guilts people into giving — He loves it when generosity flows from a heart of joy. That's because generosity is not about obligation; it's about overflow. When you truly understand how much God has given you — "He who did not spare His own Son, but gave Him up for us all, how will He not also, along with Him, graciously give us all things?" (Romans 8:32) — giving becomes the most natural response in the world.

Jesus Himself said, "It is more blessed to give than to receive" (Acts 20:35). That sounds counterintuitive in a culture that tells you to hoard, save, and look out for number one. But Jesus was never about playing it safe with what we've been given.

Generosity breaks the power of greed, loosens the grip of anxiety over finances, and positions your heart to trust God as your true provider. It's one of the most freeing things you'll ever do.

The Tithe & Beyond

In Malachi 3:10, God says, "Bring the whole tithe into the storehouse, that there may be food in my house. Test me in this," says the Lord Almighty, "and see if I will not throw open the floodgates of heaven and pour out so much blessing that there will not be room enough to store it." This is one of the only places in Scripture where God actually invites us to test Him. The tithe — 10% of your income — is the biblical starting point, not the ceiling.

It's an act of faith that declares, "God, You are my provider, not my paycheck.".

We know that for many young adults, money is tight. Student loans, rent, entry-level salaries — it's real, and we don't minimize that. But tithing isn't about what makes sense on a spreadsheet; it's about trusting God with what He's already given you.

Beyond the tithe, offerings and spontaneous generosity to those in need reflect the very heart of Christ, who "though He was rich, yet for your sake He became poor, so that you through His poverty might become rich" (2 Corinthians 8:9). Start where you are. Be faithful with what you have.

Watch God move.

Where Your Giving Goes

Every dollar you give is stewarded with integrity, transparency, and prayerful intentionality. We take this responsibility seriously because it's not our money — it's God's, and you've trusted us to manage it well. Your generosity directly funds our weekly gatherings and worship experiences, creating a space where people encounter Jesus every single week.

It fuels community outreach and local missions that bring hope to the hurting and the gospel to those who haven't heard it.

Your giving also makes possible young adult retreats and conferences where lives are transformed and lifelong friendships are built. It supports our benevolence fund, which provides real financial relief to individuals and families walking through crisis — because the church should be the first place people turn when life falls apart, not the last. And it resources discipleship and growth tools — study materials, mentorship programs, and leadership development — that help every person in our community take their next step with Jesus.

When you give here, you're investing in lives that will echo into eternity.

Generosity as Worship

Here's something that might reframe everything: giving is not a transaction — it's an act of worship. Philippians 4:18 describes financial gifts as "a fragrant offering, an acceptable sacrifice, pleasing to God." When the church at Philippi sent money to support Paul's ministry, the Holy Spirit didn't call it a donation or a contribution — He called it a sacrifice that was fragrant and pleasing to the Lord. That means every time you give, heaven takes notice.

When you give, you're declaring something profound: that God is bigger than your bank account, that His Kingdom is worth investing in, and that your security is found in Him alone — not in a number on a screen. Generosity rewires your heart. It teaches you to hold things loosely and people tightly.

It aligns your priorities with the priorities of heaven. And in a generation drowning in financial anxiety and consumer culture, choosing radical generosity is one of the most countercultural, Christ-like things you can do. Let your giving be your worship.
